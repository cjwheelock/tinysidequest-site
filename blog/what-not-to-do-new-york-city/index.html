What Not to Do in New York City

By SideQuest·October 8, 2026

New York is full of things worth stopping for. The trick is choosing where to stop. Here are four visitor habits to skip, with small alternatives that leave more room for the city and the people sharing it.

Think of this as a friendly field guide for your next outing with someone. No speed test, secret handshake, or audition for honorary New Yorker required. Bring your curiosity and a little awareness of what's happening around you.

1. Don't turn the sidewalk into your planning meeting

The map says left. Your friend says right. Everyone stops to inspect the evidence. Congratulations: your group has become a very small traffic island.

Before checking directions or framing a photo, find a place to pause out of the flow, without blocking a doorway, curb ramp, or crossing. Keep room for people to pass when moving together, too. If space narrows, let the group narrow with it.

Everyone gets to move at their own pace. If you're passing, be patient and give space. WNYC's sidewalk discussion includes both complaints about blocked paths and reminders to consider people with different mobility needs. Courtesy goes both ways.

2. Don't make the subway doorway your headquarters

A subway door is an entrance and an exit, which is a lot of responsibility for one rectangle.

Stand beside the doors and let people get off first. Once aboard, move farther into the car when you can safely do so. Give priority to riders who need accessible seats, and keep your bag off the neighboring seat. The MTA's riding guide recommends these boarding habits and says not to hold doors open. If your friend misses the train, regroup afterward rather than trying to wrestle the doors into submission.

3. Don't pick a train by color alone

Matching colors on the subway map don't guarantee matching stops. Check the train's letter or number, direction, and whether it serves your destination. In Manhattan, uptown means north and downtown means south; read the platform signs and the train's destination, too.

Before leaving, check current service information. The MTA explains how to find the right train and watch for service notices. If your route depends on an elevator, check elevator status as well. A minute spent checking can save an unplanned transit adventure.

4. Don't let Midtown become your whole New York

Keep the landmark you've been excited to see. Then leave space for one neighborhood outing instead of trying to squeeze a second entire itinerary into the afternoon.

For example, NYC Tourism highlights Jackson Heights in Queens for its restaurants, markets, and bakeries along Roosevelt Avenue and 74th Street. Browse the guide, choose a manageable area, and check your route before heading over. Agree on time, access needs, and any spending together.

Once there, notice the place without turning everyday life into a photo shoot. Leave entrances clear, ask before photographing someone, and give people room to work or simply go about their day.

Your optional side quest: the three-detail swap

With a friend, choose a short route you can comfortably walk or roll, or a public seated spot. Each collect three observations: an interesting building detail, a color you hadn't noticed, and a public sign that raises a question. No photographing strangers or entering private spaces needed.

Pause somewhere out of the way and compare finds. Give yourselves one point for each different detail you noticed, then invent a three-word title for the outing. The prize is a new conversation.

That is the kind of small adventure SideQuest encourages: getting outside, being creative, and sharing a spontaneous discovery with someone. Give the city your attention, and leave everyone else a little room.
