What Not to Do in Philadelphia

By SideQuest·October 18, 2026

Philadelphia gives you plenty to talk about. Save the lengthy sandwich deliberations for somewhere other than the front of the line.

Bring a friend, choose a manageable area, and leave time to notice something beyond the familiar photo stops. These three habits are worth skipping on your next Philly outing.

1. Don't convene a cheesesteak jury at the counter

You can have strong cheese opinions and still be ready when it's your turn.

Visit Philadelphia's cheesesteak guide suggests deciding your cheese and onion choices before reaching the counter. It explains the shorthand you may hear: ‘wit’ means with onions; ‘whiz wit’ asks for Cheez Whiz and grilled onions.

That shorthand is useful context, not a citywide language test. Read the particular shop's menu, follow its ordering setup, and ask a clear question if you need help. Plain words work for explaining what you want. Give staff time to answer, and move along when you're finished so the next person can order.

Keep room in the outing for other interests, too. Reading Terminal Market's official directory includes bakeries, eateries, produce, flowers, and more. Browse together and choose something that fits your appetites and budget; check the merchant's current details before setting your heart on a particular stop.

Once you've found somewhere to pause, compare your discoveries. Nobody needs to win lunch.

2. Don't let the famous photo stops be the whole visit

Take the picture you came for. Then make space for a different kind of looking.

Mural Arts Philadelphia's tour information includes a self-guided option for Center City murals, with a link to its map. Use it to find a small area you'd enjoy exploring together. Check the actual distance, crossings, and access needs before choosing your route.

At a mural, try looking for the artist's name or the work's title before lining up your camera. Read any available project information. You can enjoy the colors and composition while also being curious about the people and ideas behind the artwork.

Choose a public viewing spot that leaves room for others. Keep doorways and curb ramps clear, stay out of private property, and ask before photographing a person. If a good view requires standing in the street, choose another view.

Two works and a conversation can fill your outing. Let each person choose one detail to point out, and give your friend time to see it. There is no bonus for collecting every mural pin.

3. Don't turn SEPTA's doors into your planning desk

Check the journey before boarding so your map consultation doesn't become a group huddle in the entrance.

SEPTA's How to Ride Guide links trip-planning resources and explains how its different services work. For Metro trips, it advises following directional signs to the correct platform, staying behind the yellow line, and letting passengers off before boarding.

Its Metro etiquette also calls for clear aisles and doorways, yielding designated seats to older and disabled riders, and using headphones. Keep the afternoon's highlights between you and your friend rather than broadcasting them through a phone speaker.

Check current service information and your group's access needs before setting out. Leave enough time to navigate without rushing, and ask SEPTA staff for guidance if you're unsure. Getting there comfortably counts as part of the outing.

Your optional side quest: find a shared color

Choose a color together. On a short public stretch, each spot three examples in buildings, signs, plants, or public artwork. Award one point for every example your friend hadn't noticed.

Pause somewhere clear of traffic to compare your finds, then invent a name for your tiny color collection. A seated version works, too. Keep strangers and private spaces out of the game, and let everyone move at their own pace.

SideQuest encourages getting outside, being creative and spontaneous, and interacting with people in the real world. A shared outing can start with one place, one friend, and something you notice together.
