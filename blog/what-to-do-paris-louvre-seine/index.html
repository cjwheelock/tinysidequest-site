What to Do in Paris: Three Places to Pause Around the Louvre and Seine

By SideQuest·November 4, 2026

Wondering what to do in Paris between the museum booking and dinner? Give the spaces around the landmarks their own place in the day. Cour Carrée, the Palais-Royal garden and Place Dauphine offer three different ways to enjoy central Paris without making every stop an indoor attraction.

Think of this as an afternoon-sized selection. It leaves time for a courtyard, a garden and a square on the Seine’s island, with room to notice how each place changes the pace of the city.

1. Look at the Louvre from Cour Carrée

The Louvre is more than the moment you enter the museum. Cour Carrée gives the palace’s exterior a space of its own, surrounded by stone façades. The Louvre’s official visiting page lists courtyard hours separately from museum opening hours, an important distinction when planning a walk.

Let the architecture occupy your attention before deciding whether to take a picture. There is plenty here for someone who enjoys the shape and atmosphere of a place without needing a lesson in every part of its history. From a comfortable position, the courtyard can feel like an encounter with the building rather than a queue on the way inside.

Plan the stop: Use an open public entrance. If courtyard access is restricted, a lawful exterior view from Rue de l’Amiral de Coligny keeps the visit outdoors. A courtyard visit does not include museum admission; book an interior visit separately if you want one.

2. Give the Palais-Royal garden a little time

North of the Louvre, the Palais-Royal garden offers a change in scale. Paths, planting and surrounding architecture make this a place to linger rather than simply pass through. France’s Ministry of Culture records it as a Jardin remarquable.

Arrive without needing the garden to be empty or in full bloom. A useful pause can come from the relationship between the planted space and the buildings around it. If you are travelling together, leave enough time for each person to enjoy the garden at their own pace.

Plan the stop: Enter by an open, signed garden entrance and check the posted closing time. Garden access is different from access to the surrounding institutions and businesses. You can enjoy the public paths without making a shopping or café stop.

3. Find a different Paris at Place Dauphine

Place Dauphine sits on the Île de la Cité near Pont Neuf. Its triangular layout makes it feel distinct from the broad spaces around the Louvre. The City of Paris guide describes the square’s layout and public access.

This is a good final stop for an outing that has gradually moved toward the river. Take in the square from its public space, with no need to turn the visit into a restaurant reservation. The pleasure can be as simple as having time to stay after the first impression.

Plan a manageable central Paris outing

Start at the Palais-Royal garden, continue to the Louvre, then make your way toward Pont Neuf and Place Dauphine. Allow about two to three hours for an unhurried outing, including pauses. Use street crossings and check your route if stairs or uneven paving are a concern.

These suggestions focus on outdoor public spaces. Paid museum visits, food and transport are separate. Garden and courtyard access can change for weather, security or events, so check the linked official information before your visit. Paris does not have to deliver a cloudless day or an empty square for this plan to work.
