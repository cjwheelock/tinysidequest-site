Stop Waiting for the Perfect Plan: Try a Small Adventure Together

By SideQuest·October 6, 2026

To turn “we should do something” into an actual afternoon together, choose one place, one time, and one tiny mission. Leave enough room for the unplanned part.

Maybe you want to catch up with a friend. Maybe you've met someone at a class and would like a reason to meet again. Either way, the next plan can be small enough to say yes to without rearranging a whole weekend.

SideQuest's purpose is to encourage getting outside, being creative and spontaneous, and interacting with people in the real world. A small shared adventure is one way to put that purpose into practice.

Make the first decision easy

Choose a starting point both of you can reach, then suggest a time and a rough finish. A short outing leaves fewer details to negotiate.

You might meet outside a library, at an accessible park entrance, or at a neighborhood public square. Pick somewhere familiar enough that the logistics don't become the main event. If entry, reservations, or purchases are involved, check the current details and agree on a budget first.

Give the outing a tiny mission

The mission is a conversation starter. Choose something that invites each person to notice or make a different thing.

  • The detail swap: each find one detail the other person missed. Pause somewhere out of the way and show each other.
  • The three-word postcard: choose three words for the place you're in. Compare your choices and find one detail that explains them.
  • The two-minute sketch: draw the same object, then explain what caught your attention. Drawing skill is optional.
  • The curiosity stop: read a public sign together and each pick one question you'd like to investigate later.

These are DIY ideas, with no points or purchases required. Use a route you can walk or roll comfortably, or choose a seated spot where you can both participate.

Keep some of the afternoon open

Give yourself one planned starting point and one small goal. After that, see what catches your attention.

A public artwork might invite a closer look. A shop window might remind someone of a story. You might decide that sitting and talking is the best part of the plan. Change direction together, keeping your time, access needs, and spending limit in mind.

You don't have to collect a checklist of stops. Finding one thing worth sharing is enough.

Try the plan with someone new

If you'd like to meet people, start with an activity that already brings participants together. Check a library calendar, a neighborhood organization, or the organizer of a public creative workshop. Choose something you're interested in and learn how participation works.

Start a conversation about what you're both doing. A question about a drawing, an activity, or a shared discovery gives you a place to begin. If you both enjoy talking, suggest another short outing. Let the invitation be easy to accept or decline.

Make the follow-up concrete

At the end, say what you enjoyed and suggest one possible next time. A specific invitation is easier to act on than another open-ended promise to hang out.

SideQuest's quests can help supply a prompt when you want an idea for the next outing. You can also start with 21 real-life side quest ideas or the seven-day challenge and choose one to share.

Your next adventure can begin with a small decision: pick a time, invite someone, and give curiosity somewhere to go.
