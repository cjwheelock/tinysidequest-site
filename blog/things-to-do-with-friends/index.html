15 Things to Do With Friends When Nobody Can Decide

By SideQuest·September 21, 2026

When nobody can decide what to do with friends, agree on time and budget first, then choose between two concrete plans. A short activity with a clear finish is easier to accept than an open-ended request to “hang out sometime.”

Use the fifteen ideas below as starting points. Change the location, distance, or activity so everyone can take part. Time suggestions describe the activity, not travel.

Make the decision in three steps

  1. Set the limits. Ask how much time people have, what they want to spend, and whether the plan needs to be indoors.
  2. Offer two choices. “Thirty-minute photo hunt or a library browse?” gives the group something specific to answer.
  3. Choose a finish. Agree on a return time, a number of stops, or one thing to make. Anyone can opt out or request a different plan.

Quick plans: around thirty minutes

  1. Two-person photo hunt. Choose three subjects such as an interesting shadow, unusual lettering, and a repeated shape. Photograph places or objects, then compare favorites. No purchase required.
  2. Trade a five-minute tour. Each show the other one nearby public detail you like. Give it a title instead of preparing a speech. No purchase required.
  3. Draw the same view. Bring paper you already have, choose a view, and draw for ten minutes. Show each other one detail you noticed. No skill requirement and no new purchase needed.
  4. Choose a soundtrack. Using music you already have access to, each select two songs for an imaginary journey. Listen and explain the choices. No new purchase required.
  5. Make a tiny recommendation exchange. Each bring one book, film, recipe, or place you already know and explain who might enjoy it. Finish when everyone has one new thing to look up. No purchase required.

An hour together: one place, one prompt

  1. Library shelf swap. Each choose a subject for the other to browse. Find one intriguing page and compare what you found. Use a library you can access and check its hours.
  2. Window-display awards. Choose a public shopping street and each pick a favorite display. Invent award names in your own notes. No purchase required.
  3. One-ingredient snack challenge. Agree on an ingredient already available, then make two simple variations together. Check allergies and dietary needs first. No new purchase required.
  4. Shared sketch map. Draw a familiar area from memory together, then check one uncertain detail using a map. Finish by marking a place you'd like to revisit. No purchase required.
  5. Postcard makers. Each make a postcard-sized drawing or collage from materials you have. Write a message on the back; sending it is optional. No new purchase required.

A free afternoon: a small shared adventure

  1. Three-stop neighborhood route. Choose one thing to look at, one place to sit, and one place to browse. Agree on the route and access needs together. Spending is optional; check admission and transport costs.
  2. One-room gallery visit. Choose an open museum or gallery within the group's budget. Each select a work from the same room and explain your choice. Check tickets and photography rules.
  3. Favorite-place exchange. Each contribute one nearby place to a short itinerary. Keep the number of stops small enough that nobody needs to rush. Costs depend on the places chosen.
  4. Theme picnic. Bring food you already planned to eat and choose a theme such as one color or childhood favorites. Use a suitable permitted space or eat together indoors. Keep the theme flexible.
  5. Make a one-page field guide. Explore one accessible public area and collect five sketches, observations, or photos of objects. Combine them into a page titled after the outing. No purchase required.

Keep the activity social

The prompt is there to give the time together a shape. You can stop following it once a conversation or shared interest takes over. Nobody needs to perform for a camera or earn the group's approval.

If you're turning an outing into a game, choose a shared finish rather than a leaderboard. “Find five things as a group” lets people contribute in different ways.
