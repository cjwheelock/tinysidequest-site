What Not to Do in Chicago

By SideQuest·October 12, 2026

Chicago has room for a big skyline moment and a small neighborhood discovery. Skip these four visitor habits, bring a friend, and leave space for the city to surprise you.

You don't need to pass a local knowledge exam. A little preparation, a little consideration, and a willingness to look beyond the next famous photo can make a good outing. Even the condiment debate can stay friendly.

1. Don't let the Loop become your whole Chicago

Enjoy the downtown stop you've been looking forward to. Then give one neighborhood its own unhurried part of the day. Your itinerary doesn't earn extra credit for resembling an airport departures board.

For one starting point, Choose Chicago's Pilsen guide highlights murals, galleries, and Mexican cultural institutions, including the National Museum of Mexican Art. Browse the guide and pick a manageable area rather than trying to see everything.

Check opening hours for any indoor stop and agree on travel time, access needs, and spending with your friend. View murals from public space, leave entrances clear, and ask before photographing people. Read an artwork's title or artist credit when available; learning who made something is part of noticing it.

2. Don't board the ‘L’ on vibes alone

The train looks promising. Promising is not a destination.

The CTA's riding guide explains that the ‘L’ includes elevated, subway, and ground-level tracks. Follow station signs and check the train's route and destination before boarding. The nickname doesn't mean you should always be looking up.

Stand back from the platform edge, avoid running, let passengers exit first, and move into the car when you can safely do so. Those habits come straight from the guide. Before heading out, check CTA service and elevator alerts; build your route around everyone's needs. If you get turned around, pause somewhere clear of the flow and ask a station employee for help.

3. Don't turn a hot-dog order into a loyalty test

Chicago's ketchup debate has history. Your lunch does not need a courtroom.

In WBEZ's reporting on the Chicago hot dog, a food historian describes leaving off ketchup as tradition. The same story includes a customer who disagrees and people ordering variations. That is useful context for the joke, without pretending every resident has identical tastes.

If you want to try a classic Chicago-style dog, ask what comes on it. If ingredients don't work for you, ask about options before ordering. Read the menu, have your order ready, and treat the person at the counter kindly. There's no need to provoke a reaction for a video—or to police your friend's lunch.

4. Don't make the lakefront path your photo studio

The skyline can hold a pose. Other trail users still need somewhere to go.

The Chicago Park District's Lakefront Trail page describes separate bicycle and pedestrian trails and posts current repair information. Check it before leaving, follow signs and marked routes, and avoid active work zones.

When a view catches your attention, find a safe public place clear of the travel path before stopping. Leave passing room, look both ways where paths cross, and keep your attention on your surroundings. Choose a shorter outing or a seated viewpoint if that suits your group. The lake doesn't require you to complete a mileage target.

Your optional side quest: build a tiny city soundtrack

At a comfortable public spot away from traffic, each notice three sounds: one rhythmic, one surprising, and one you would normally overlook. Listen directly; recording strangers isn't part of the game.

Compare your choices, give yourselves a point for each different sound, and invent a song title for the outing. A seated version works, too. SideQuest encourages getting outside, being creative and spontaneous, and interacting with people. One shared discovery is a fine place to begin.
