Things to Do in London: Three Stops for a Playful Afternoon

By SideQuest·November 13, 2026

Looking for things to do in London between the bookings already on your calendar? Give the South Bank an outing of its own. The National Theatre exterior, Waterloo Bridge and Jubilee Gardens offer architecture, river views and a place to pause without requiring another show ticket.

Keep the area small and let the visit unfold. The Thames is an attraction, but so are the buildings, paths and public spaces that shape the experience of being beside it.

1. See the National Theatre from outside

On the South Bank beside Queen’s Walk, the National Theatre has a strongly sculptural exterior. Its terraces and concrete forms give the building a presence beyond the performances inside. The theatre’s own location catalogue documents its architecture and setting.

Look from the open riverside public route, without needing access to a terrace or interior. This can be a satisfying stop for someone who likes buildings but does not want the afternoon to become a formal architecture tour. Let yourself enjoy the scale and setting before moving on.

Plan the stop: An exterior view needs no theatre ticket. Shows, tours and interior facilities have separate arrangements. Use public paths and respect any restricted areas or event activity.

2. Give Waterloo Bridge more than crossing time

Waterloo Bridge carries road traffic and pedestrians across the Thames. Historic England’s listing identifies the bridge and its design history. For this visit, its most immediate appeal is the change of viewpoint as you move above the river.

Use the pedestrian footway and allow the view to be part of the experience. You can enjoy a short section without making a full crossing compulsory. If you do cross, remember that your next stop is back on the South Bank.

Plan the stop: This is a working road bridge, so keep to the footway and leave room for others. Wind can make it feel colder than the riverside below. Check the route to and from the bridge if avoiding steps matters to your visit.

3. Leave a pause for Jubilee Gardens

Jubilee Gardens sits beside Queen’s Walk near the London Eye. The gardens’ visitor guide gives access information from the riverside and Belvedere Road, with Waterloo nearby.

A garden in a busy visitor area does not have to be silent to offer a worthwhile pause. Enjoy an open path, the planting and the contrast with the riverfront movement. You do not need to ride the London Eye for the gardens to belong in your afternoon.

Plan the stop: The gardens are a shared public space. The playground has its own opening times and rules, and nearby toilets may be charged. Check the visitor information if those facilities are important to your plan.

Keep the South Bank outing manageable

Begin at the National Theatre, take your chosen section of Waterloo Bridge, then return to the South Bank and continue along open riverside paths toward Jubilee Gardens. Budget roughly two to three hours for a relaxed version with pauses; that is an editorial time allowance, not a timed or access-certified route.

All three suggestions are outdoor visits without attraction admission. Transport, refreshments, performances and rides are separate. If rain or wind changes your plans, one well-chosen stop is enough. The value is in having time to enjoy London, not in finishing a circuit.
