What Not to Do in San Francisco

By SideQuest·October 10, 2026

San Francisco rewards a little flexibility: in your plans, in your layers, and in your opinions about nicknames. Skip these four visitor habits and make room for a small adventure with someone you like.

You can admire the famous views and still be a considerate guest. Start with one manageable outing, leave time to linger, and remember that the city continues being somebody's home between your photo stops.

1. Don't assume “San Fran” is the easy option

The full name works perfectly well. Give those extra syllables their moment.

KQED's reporting on city nicknames puts “San Fran” in the category that needs discussion. Treat the objection as a cultural preference, rather than a rule every resident shares. Saying “San Francisco” is a straightforward way to avoid making your introduction a vocabulary debate.

“Frisco” has a different, complicated history. The same report traces its working-class associations and interviews locals who use it proudly. Hearing someone say it isn't an invitation to correct them. Listen to how people describe their own city; you might learn something more interesting than a list of forbidden words.

2. Don't pack for a California beach montage

Your sunglasses and your jacket can both earn a place in the bag.

SF Travel's packing guide recommends layers and a light jacket or windbreaker because conditions can change by the hour and within a few blocks. A sunny start doesn't settle the question for the rest of the outing.

Check the forecast before leaving, bring something you can add or remove, and choose footwear that works for your route. Make the plan comfortable for everyone joining you. A sheltered pause or a shorter route can be a perfectly good plot twist; nobody needs to prove anything to the fog.

3. Don't give your backpack its own Muni seat

The bag has had a lovely day. It can enjoy the next part from your lap.

SFMTA's Muni guide asks riders to let others off before boarding, keep aisles clear, and keep bags off seats. Respect priority seating for seniors and people with disabilities, and use headphones for audio. Hold on or stay seated while the vehicle is moving.

Plan your trip and have your fare ready before boarding. If you're using MuniMobile, activate your ticket first. Then put the logistics away long enough to share the view with your friend, while staying aware of your stop and the people around you.

4. Don't mistake a postcard view for an empty set

The Painted Ladies are beautiful from the outside, and that is where your admiration belongs.

SF Travel's Painted Ladies visitor guide explains that these Steiner Street houses are occupied homes, across from Alamo Square Park. Enjoy the view from public space. Leave steps, driveways, and entrances clear, and keep room for other people to pass.

Try noticing one architectural detail instead of directing a lengthy photo shoot. Ask before photographing a person, keep your voices considerate, and take your rubbish with you. The neighborhood doesn't need extras; it already has neighbors.

Your optional side quest: pitch a tiny postcard

Choose a public spot where you and a friend can comfortably pause. Each pick one building detail, shape, or color you can see, then sketch it for a minute. Drawing skill is entirely optional.

Invent a title for your postcard and give your friend a twenty-second pitch for why that detail deserves it. Award one point for a surprising observation and one for a title that makes the other person smile. A seated version works just as well; keep the game away from traffic and private property.

SideQuest encourages getting outside, being creative and spontaneous, and interacting with people in the real world. A jacket, a friend, and one small thing worth noticing are a good place to start.
