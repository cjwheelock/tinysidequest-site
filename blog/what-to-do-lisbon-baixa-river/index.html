What to Do in Lisbon: Three Stops from Baixa to the River

By SideQuest·November 12, 2026

Wondering what to do in Lisbon when you want to explore without organising the day around a tram queue? Start in Baixa and work toward the river. Rossio, Praça do Município and Ribeira das Naus give you three public places where the ground, buildings and waterfront all deserve attention.

This is a small selection for a curious outing, with time to pause between the larger attractions on your list. You can enjoy it without boarding a boat or entering a monument.

1. Begin in Rossio

Rossio is the familiar name for Praça Dom Pedro IV, one of central Lisbon’s longstanding meeting places. Visit Lisboa’s Rossio guide locates it in the Pombaline downtown and describes its role in city life.

Give the square more time than it takes to cross. Its paving and surrounding façades make it a place to enjoy at ground level, with the everyday movement of the city around you. Find a comfortable public position away from entrances and traffic before stopping.

Plan the stop: Rossio is a public square, not a ticketed attraction. Cafés and shops are optional. Be attentive to crossings around its edges, and take extra care on smooth paving when it is wet.

2. Look around Praça do Município

Praça do Município gives this outing a second square with a different setting, facing Lisbon’s city hall. The city’s decorative paving is worth noticing here; the official Portuguese Paving Route offers context for this craft across Lisbon.

Enjoy the public pavement and the relationship between the square and its civic façade. You do not need to turn the stop into an architectural quiz. A smaller square can reward the same patience you might ordinarily reserve for a major attraction.

Plan the stop: This is an exterior visit. Access to city hall is separate and not part of the suggestion. Leave official entrances clear and follow any event or security restrictions affecting the square.

3. End with the Tagus at Ribeira das Naus

Ribeira das Naus is a riverside public space between the Praça do Comércio area and Cais do Sodré. Visit Lisboa describes it as a former boatyard transformed into a place to spend time beside the water. Its visitor guide also makes clear that bathing is prohibited.

Use the upper promenade for a comfortable view. There is no need to go down toward the water to enjoy the broad change from Baixa’s streets to the river. Leave a little unscheduled time here, rather than treating the waterfront as a backdrop for the next appointment.

Plan the stop: Stay on open public paving and away from wet edges. A river view does not require a ferry fare or a café purchase. Wind, rain and bright sun can all change how long you want to linger.

Plan the journey from square to waterfront

Start at Rossio, continue through Baixa to Praça do Município, then reach Ribeira das Naus by open streets and designated crossings. Set aside about two to three hours as a flexible planning budget including pauses. It is an editorial estimate, not a measured route or a promise of step-free travel.

Use a current map to choose connections that suit your mobility and the conditions that day. These three public-space stops need no attraction tickets; transport, food and optional visits cost extra. You can finish at the river without adding a hilltop viewpoint simply because it appears on another itinerary.
